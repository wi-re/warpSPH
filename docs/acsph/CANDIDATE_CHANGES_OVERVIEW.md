## 3. What the measured wall times can and cannot say

Marrone 3.1 nx 70, tLimit 2.45, video on, per-step forensics on, CUDA graphs off, and **the GPU shared with an LM Studio
`llama-server` (about 63 GB, 89 % utilisation) for the later runs**:

| Run (all-aligned unless noted) | steps | wall s | ms/step |
|---|---|---|---|
| all on | 2446 | 1412 | 577 |
| RK2 (`rk` off) | 2341 | 1220 | 521 |
| acceleration constraint on | 2445 | 1330 | 544 |
| per-iteration detection off | 2371 | 1300 | 548 |
| Δt/Δτ 5, CFL_t 0.2 | 4704 | 3790 | 806 |
| λ taper off | 2352 | 1845 | 784 (GPU likely shared) |
| D1 off | 2557 | 1760 | 688 (GPU likely shared) |

The spread inside one configuration (521-577 for near-identical work, 784 when the GPU was shared) is larger than any
single flag's expected effect, and the 25 % GPU utilisation the runs showed means the time is host launch overhead, not
kernel work. So **per-flag prices must come from a controlled micro-benchmark on an idle GPU**, per-stage and
per-iteration, graph on and off (`benchmarks/marrone31/bench_step.py` splits a step into integrator / hook / diagnostics
with launches and syncs). That is the first task of the optimisation pass, after the attribution is finished.

## 4. What the evidence suggests (to be confirmed by the D5-only run)

The failing runs show the fluid ending up 4-5 dx inside the wall (`maxPenetrationDx`) right before the blow-up, and the one
piece whose removal breaks the all-aligned run is the position-row no-penetration device. That points at the position row
(`Dx/Dτ = ṽ`) as the leak: the paper constrains wall particles' `ṽ` but nothing stops a *fluid* particle's own `ṽ` from
carrying it through the wall between detections. If the D5-only run survives, the principled version of D5 is a
no-penetration constraint on the position update itself (a physics-derived criterion, not a threshold), and the
authors' bounce-back is one cheap way of imposing it.
